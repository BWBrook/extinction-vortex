# Caribou source-recovery review, 14 October 2026

Astra review of sections 0–3 and 6 of Fable's [recovery matrix](option3_recovery_matrix_20261013.md), at checkout `20c8574`. This supports Barry's narrowed deep dives alongside the core paper. It does not select a new empirical design or run a model. Haley Lake is reviewed separately.

**Finding:** the new public sources materially improve the caribou cases. They support descriptive crosswalks and explicitly conditional sensitivity illustrations now. They do not yet provide an observation-resolved, source-specific test identifying abundance feedback. Replace the categorical verdict “census usable; rates need transfer” with that distinction. Rate transfer is one possible modelling choice, not a prerequisite for a useful deep dive.

## Census checks

**Maligne.** I re-downloaded the [Parks Canada CSV](https://open.canada.ca/data/dataset/8d2849d9-a723-46c1-b94b-ed4a76e78195/resource/f9a392dc-aa09-41bd-ae74-c8de9ed5d42b/download/jasper_np_alpine_caribou_population_1988-2017_data.csv): SHA-256 `623bb71d011721c7dc59254d7eb1cf6c849d92bae0579d2b7671f350afc43c83`, matching the matrix. It has 525 observations after two language header rows; 99 are Maligne (86 rut, nine recruitment, four scat). All 21 fall-year sums reproduce the matrix, including 2001 = 21, 2003 = 24, 2004 = 11, 2008 = 6 and 2014 = 4. These sums reproduce the file, not an adjudicated population size.

Some earlier rows are identical except for unique ID: `144/148/149` (1993), `166/167` (1996), `189/190` (1998). Discrete one-male group records can coincide in fields, particularly when location is missing or broad. Deleting these would reduce independently matching annual totals. Thus neither whole-row similarity nor matching composition is a safe deduplication rule. A group-observation record has no individual identity with which to resolve repeat sightings.

The source has more composition information than a total-only trajectory. Calf versus non-calf data can inform recruitment conditional on census timing and classification. They share a sampling event and denominator with abundance, so a joint likelihood or conditional composition analysis is appropriate; counting the same total twice as independent evidence is not. This dependence does not make the composition automatically unusable. The archive's `dataprep.Rmd` also subtracts unclassified life stage/sex from recruitment denominators: “non-calf denominator” should therefore be read as classified non-calves under that construction, not necessarily every non-calf animal present.

**Purcells Central.** The public WSI query returns 92 unsecured summary rows, 25 with parameter `Individuals`. The critical crosswalk independently reproduces:

The 1997 value two is a real observed count, not a presumed transcription error. Equally, its subsequent increase cannot be read as population recovery without accounting for coverage. The first zero sighting in 2005 is not the same event as a zero MNKA/official estimate in 2006, and neither is an observed last death. WSI also flags 2003 as two partial surveys; its MC value seven conflicts with the note mentioning ten. Preserve this conflict.

Several years have both OTC and MC flagged `BEST_PARAMETER_VALUE=Yes` (1998, 2000, 2002, 2003), so that field alone cannot select one unique annual abundance value. The general note says reports before 2006 summarized South Purcells before the two named herds were split. That is a crosswalk caution, not proof that every pre-2006 row is assigned incorrectly. Receipt entries retain row IDs, dates, methods and notes. The query URL is recorded in the manifest; geometry is ignored by the checks.

## Demographic evidence and causal scope

The [pinned preprocessing code](https://github.com/ctlamb/CaribouIPM_BCAB/blob/f069f1f6f0705dc954a30a4cecbd9ff9bba7f2e3/data/dataprep.Rmd) uses `survfit(Surv(enter, exit, event) ~ year)` evaluated at month 12, with staggered monitoring. It assigns its reported standard error to `sd`, drops recorded n ≤ 2, and constructs boundary uncertainty by resampling an artificial vector containing one failure and n−1 successes. Thus `sqrt(p(1-p)/n)` inversion does not recover the denominator of these KM or boundary estimates. Published collar counts corroborate small samples but do not supply each annual risk set. A reported mean of one with positive constructed uncertainty also cannot simply parameterize an ordinary beta distribution by exact moment matching.

Those summaries are still source-specific demographic evidence, albeit sparse and processed. They can bound a sensitivity illustration without pretending raw likelihoods have been recovered. Extrapolating to unobserved years, choosing female proportions or fertility, or importing rates requires assumptions that should be visible. None is mandatory for a source-led descriptive deep dive. The fitted IPM posteriors reuse the counts and rates and cannot be relabelled independent calibration data.

The matrix overstates the external-driver argument. Rates measured during decline may reflect environmental deterioration, abundance feedback, sampling variation or combinations of these. They do not independently identify a feedback-free counterfactual, but nor does the timing prove feedback exists. A closed population can decline under density-independent low survival or recruitment; closure concerns movement. A particular fixed-rate null may be inadequate for changing predation pressure, whereas an explicitly exogenous time-varying alternative could represent it. Neither discrepancy alone establishes an extinction vortex.

For Banff, [Hebblewhite, White and Musiani's primary paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/38024114_Revisiting_Extinction_in_National_Parks_Mountain_Caribou_in_Banff) describes aerial surveys, annual maxima and telemetry in Figure 1, extending to extirpation in 2009. It reports the avalanche killing the final five animals and notes preceding recruitment failure. This supports a catastrophe-and-monitoring case study. It does not resolve the archive's 2008 zero, recover individual rates, or demonstrate a density-feedback mechanism. Its described elk management changes the environment; that is distinct from adding or removing focal caribou.

## What the public sources support now

| Case | Useful present contribution | Boundary before a feedback test |
|---|---|---|
| Maligne | Dated count/composition crosswalk; disappearance chronology; conditional illustrations using sparse herd-specific survival summaries | Resolve or explicitly bracket competing counts, group identity, observation model, temporal coverage and endpoint interpretation |
| Purcells Central | Particularly clear demonstration of OTC/MNKA/MC differences, incomplete coverage and changing endpoint conventions; sparse rate-informed sensitivity | Define geographic unit and observation process; preserve track-based evidence and uncertainty; justify missing demographic periods |
| Banff | Count-series crosswalk, management context and documented catastrophic endpoint | Explain year labels; avoid treating a shared catastrophe as independent individual mortality or identifying feedback from the trajectory |

Palmer does not require another search for this tranche. The matrix's documented Humble Island banding cohort is useful additional provenance, but an approximate release cohort plus positive sightings does not alone provide island-specific capture histories, effort or survival. It remains a distinct open breeding-population comparator if retained.
