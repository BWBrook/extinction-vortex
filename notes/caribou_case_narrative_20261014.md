# Case study: three mountain caribou herds (draft manuscript text)

Fable, 14 October 2026. Descriptive deep dive, accepted in board 1625.

**Artefacts** (`analysis/fable/caribou/`):
- figure: `caribou_case.{png,pdf}`;
- tables: `maligne_census.csv`, `purcells_central_census.csv`,
  `banff_census.csv`, `demographic_evidence.csv` and `events_timeline.csv`;
- register: `conflict_register.md` (A1–A10, B1–B6, C);
- sources: `caribou_sources.json`.

It contains no test, fitted reconstruction or new interval. Caribou are not
among the nine Fagan–Holmes series; the case is illustrative.

---

Three southern mountain caribou (*Rangifer tarandus*) herds that disappeared
in the 2000s and 2010s add a second, contrasting view of what lies behind an
extinction-bound count. Two appear in the Williams et al. (2021) compilation:
- Banff (ID 28), whose 16 annual values equal the Lamb et al. (2024) archive
  input exactly;
- very probably Maligne (ID 27), which matches that archive in 1998 and
  2013–2018 but not in 2006–2012 (register A2).

The third, Purcells Central in British Columbia, is not in the compilation.
It is included because its public survey record separates the kinds of
"count" that a single series merges.

**What a count is.** The three herds' series are built differently.
- **Purcells Central.** The provincial inventory records, for each survey
  (mostly March–April; 1 May in 1998; day unrecorded in 2003 and 2005), an
  observed total count (OTC), a minimum number known alive (MNKA) from tracks
  and collars, and sometimes a model-corrected estimate (MC) (Fig. Yb). Its
  general notes flag incomplete coverage in 1996, 2003 and 2005.
  - In 1997 the observed count was 2, but 14 animals were known alive: twelve
    were seen outside the survey area and both collars were found after the
    flight (A5).
  - The archive's single series uses MNKA in four years and OTC in the others
    (A6).
  - The first zero sighting (2005, with one animal inferred from tracks) is
    not the official zero (2006), and neither is a recorded last death.
- **Maligne.** The open Parks Canada data are group-level fall minimum counts
  (Fig. Ya). Their flight sums differ from the archive in six years, and the archive has no 2010 value (A1, A4).
  One 2014 pair of groups with identical composition comes from different
  locations, so we cannot tell a repeat sighting from two groups (A3).
- **Banff.** Annual maximum counts, supplemented by telemetry.

Each convention is defensible on its own terms. But a model fitted to "the"
count of these herds would inherit a choice that is rarely visible in the
series.

**How the herds ended.**
- **Banff.** The last five animals died in an avalanche in April 2009, after
  a period of recruitment failure (Hebblewhite et al. 2010). The archived
  series nonetheless records its zero in 2008 (A9).
- **Maligne.** The herd fell from 68 counted in 1998 to 3 in 2015–2017, and
  then to zero.
- **Purcells Central.** The herd fell from about 15 in 1994 to zero by 2006.

The Banff end is a known catastrophe: a proximate event that says nothing
about whether abundance feedback operated earlier.

**Documented management during the represented decline.** These actions
changed the environment rather than adding or removing caribou.
- **Banff.** Around 2002, managers reduced elk in caribou summer range,
  deferred prescribed fires and reduced human impacts.
- **Maligne.** Road measures were tried in 2005–2007, and winter access to
  the range has been closed since 2014.
- **Purcells Central.** A 2012 augmentation (an addition) is recorded under
  the neighbouring Purcells South unit; it post-dates the zero, and its
  historical unit identity is unresolved (A10).
- **Archive gaps.** The archive's six recovery-action categories are zero for
  all three herds. That records the absence of those particular actions, not
  an unmanaged population.

**What demographic evidence exists.** It is sparse and processed.
- **Survival.** Herd-year adult-female survival estimates exist for Maligne
  (2002–2007) and Purcells Central (1994–1996). They are Kaplan–Meier values
  from small collared samples, with constructed uncertainty at the boundary
  of 1. The annual risk sets were not recovered in the inspected sources (B1, B2).
- **Calf composition.** Calves per classified non-calf are available from the
  shared monitoring programme as the counts. That is not automatically a calf:cow ratio, and
  not independent of the counts.
- **Banff:** the inspected archive contains no survival or composition rows.
- **Individual fate histories** were not found in the sources we inspected.
- **IPM outputs.** The archive's integrated population model outputs reuse
  the counts and cannot serve as independent priors.

**What the case can and cannot show.** These records show why each
trajectory, taken alone, cannot identify abundance feedback.
- The counts are of different kinds and change class within a series.
- One herd ended in a single catastrophe.
- Management changed the environment during the decline.
- Survival was observed only in short windows, from few animals, and was
  measured during the decline. It may contain feedback, environmental change
  or chance, and it is not a no-feedback baseline.

Together with the Haley Lake marmots, the caribou records show that
the information needed to interpret a terminal decline exists unevenly. Some
of it is in the same field programmes, some in management records, and some
was not recovered in the inspected public sources.
