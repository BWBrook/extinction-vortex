Key. Access (per Astra): PDF = relevant full-text pages inspected; HTML = full article or chapter text read; AB = abstract only; ledger = Holmes et al. 2005 Table A-1 compilation metadata, not field methods. Demographic classes: **IND** individual-based histories; **COMP** shared-survey composition (from the same surveys as the counts); **CD** count-derived summaries (re-expressions of the counts, circular as independent priors); **TRANS** transferred (other population or species). W = Williams et al. local CSV ID. "Match" means deposited values equal an inspected source table or figure; it does not mean the historical estimator was reproduced (Stage B not passed). Shared monitoring is not itself circularity: IND and COMP add information absent from annual totals but share individuals, years and observation error with them, so they are not independent likelihoods.

## Notes

**1. Wild dog.**
- Ginsberg et al. 1995 (doi 10.1098/rspb.1995.0199) is the original study (8 October audit); its count table was not matched. MARSS file: pinned revision `bf06ca8e`, `wilddogs.rda`, SHA-256 `cb6e0620…`; comparison in `analysis/astra/source_audit/results/wilddog_comparison.csv` (MARSS missing 1971, 1972, 1978; all other 1970–1991 values equal W14). Holmes 2005 Table A-1, p. 28, labels adults + yearlings. Jackson et al. 2019 (HTML) supplies the 106/121 figure (2006–2016 handled dogs, 67 translocated), the persistence outside the study area and the later connectivity.
- F&H report one interpolation without naming the year; starting in 1973 would give their 20-year window with only the 1978 gap, "a hypothesis, not recovered provenance" (8 October audit). Protocol §7 sensitivity: treat 1971, 1972 and 1978 as missing.
- [first-pass, unverified]: Ginsberg et al. and Burrows et al. fit Monte Carlo models to the same counts, so parameters taken from them would be count-derived. A secondary source quoted about 95 dogs in 12 packs in 1970 against 77 in W14 (unreconciled). Transfer candidates (Selous, Laikipia) are in scan §1b; none verified.

**2. Wild dog handling [first-pass, unverified].** The scan reports immobilisation, radio-collaring and vaccination of study packs from the mid-1980s; Burrows et al. 1994 (AB) reports shorter adult longevity and pack life once routine intervention began, with survival differing by intervention category; via Jackson 2019 (web-fetch summary) five packs were lost 2–5 months after collar handling (1985–90) and the other seven 5–12 months after 1990 vaccination. The scan itself records the dispute: Woodroffe 2001 (AB) concludes intervention was extremely unlikely to have caused the extinction. Astra's audit neither confirms nor dates these events; it states only that handling alone does not establish opening or the cause of disappearance.

**3. Middle-spotted woodpecker.** Pettersson 1985 abstract (Biol. Conserv.): 15–20 pairs, census 1967–1974, decline 1975–1983, little change in habitat area 1941–1978, winter-loss associations, low reproduction, inbreeding proposed as an explanation (13 October audit, finding 4). The original figure or table was not obtained. [first-pass, unverified] Spanish adult survival, post-fledging and nest estimates are in scan §3b (SE or SD not stated); Nilsson et al. 1992 is cited there for last pairs in 1982, another source for 1983.

**4. Red-cockaded woodpecker.** Ligon et al. 1986, pp. 849–850 (AOU report): about 40 adults at Tall Timbers in 1970 and disappearance by 1981, citing Baker 1983; annual burning; explicit caution on attributing local decline to habitat alteration. Deposited 1970 value is 39. [first-pass, unverified] Cox & McCormick 2016 is cited in the scan for 11 potential breeding groups in 1970, nearest group more than 5 km away, and no translocation or supplementation before 1998; rates in scan §4b are from other populations.

**5. Golden plover.** Parr 1992, Ornis Scand. 23:152–158 (doi 10.2307/3676443), abstract only: return rates and winter losses that may include mortality or emigration. Parr 1993 (Bird Study 40; doi 10.1080/00063659309477187), downloaded PDF, methods and Tables 3–4 inspected: crows and gulls removed 1986–1989; no young hatched in those years; Table 3 counts pairs. Kerloch is named in Holmes Table A-1. BTO 0.73 is a species-level figure the audit did not verify as independent [first-pass, unverified]. Protocol §7 includes a sensitivity treating runs of identical counts as missing except the first.

**6. Whooping crane.** CWS/USFWS 2007 plan, Table 1, p. 13 (PDF SHA-256 `48460931…`); comparison and components in `analysis/astra/source_audit/results/crane_comparison.csv`. The earlier F&H "final 2" against deposited 1 is resolved by F&H's first-count-at-or-below-one rule (preceding count 2; 8 October audit, finding 1). Protocol §7 sensitivity: crane excluded (experimental flock with intervention history).
- [first-pass, unverified] dates: cross-fostering of eggs under sandhill cranes about 1975–1988/89; predator control at Grays Lake 1976/77–1989; guide-bird trials 1993–94; ultralight-led trials from 1996–97 and an ultralight-raised female joining in 1999. Astra confirms ultralight birds in 1997 but not these dates; the 1997 and 1999 entries are not reconciled.
- [first-pass, unverified] F&H Table 1 cites T. Stehn (USFWS), personal communication, as the count source, so the plan table is a matched identification rather than the cited source.

**7. Hawaiian crow.** USFWS 2009 plan (PDF SHA-256 `fd9cdc40…`): wild remnant pp. I-14–I-15 and Fig. 2 (visually checked 8 October; not an independent digitisation), releases pp. I-19–I-21. NRC 1992 (HTML), Box Table 2 and text, pp. 31–35: 22/27 and 0.90 are summaries of five banded McCandless adults, "not the 1992–2003 decline fit". The plan's 0.798 corresponds to its reported log-count slope; no regression was fitted in either audit. [first-pass, unverified] 17 eggs removed 1993–94 (Kuehler et al. 1995, AB) and 27 captive-reared juveniles released 1993–98 (plan p. I-19); 12 birds in 1992 including one juvenile (plan, citing Engbring 1992).

**8. Wood turtles A and B.** Garber & Burger 1995 abstract (Rutgers record): two allopatric populations in a protected watershed, mark–recapture followed 133 turtles, declines associated with public access; the abstract does not identify the fate of disappearing turtles (13 October audit, finding 4). Holmes ledger maxima 51 (A) and 58 (B) equal the deposited W8 and W9 maxima. The first-pass claim that same-population data are "fully circular" with the counts is withdrawn by Astra's audit: a survival analysis of the marked histories would not automatically equal the annual totals. Species-level survival leads are in scan §8/9b; none verified.

- Count convention: highest annual count separately for each age class, mostly before 11:00, pups after emergence (p. 14); intensive colonies include all marked animals seen through the season (p. 37); more than ten visits a year, effort higher after 1992 (p. 34); about 44% of sampled tagged adults detected on one visit (study-wide, not a Haley detection estimate). The method is documented for Bryant's record, not verified for the Williams-only years.
- Appendix 12 (p. 117), non-pups, transitions 1987→88 to 1996→97: at risk 3, 13, 12, 9, 11, 10, 9, 15, 10, 5; survivors 3, 8, 9, 8, 5, 8, 4, 10, 5, 4 (97 and 64). Printed annual mean 0.69, SD 0.19 describe variation among years, not a CI. Survivors are adults aged 2+ at t+1; minimum persistence (p. 18): missed animals, tag loss and permanent emigration look like deaths.
- Appendix 11 (p. 116), tagged pups, transitions from 1990, 1991, 1993, 1994, 1996: at risk 2, 3, 6, 6, 7; subsequently observed as tagged yearlings 2, 0, 6, 3, 0 (24 and 11). None of the tagged pups in the 1991 and 1996 cohorts was observed as a yearling in the following year (0/3, 0/7). Count-derived: 83 apparent survivors from 123 non-pup exposures; 30 from 55 pup exposures (seven nonzero cohorts).
- Pooled context, not Haley-only: Table 2, p. 44, CJS adult apparent survival 0.623 (0.565–0.678) with recapture fixed at 1, 0.629 (0.570–0.683) with estimated resighting. Bryant & Page 2005, pp. 675–678 (full-text web extraction; not re-audited 14 October): known-fate 0.74 (0.65–0.81), seasonal model 0.76 (0.68–0.83), 1992–2004 telemetry, 15 colonies including Haley, 166 marmot-site-year records; eight animals vanished with signals, and alternative coding gives 0.72.
- Conflicts (register A1–A7, B1–B6 in `analysis/fable/haley/conflict_register.md`, kept unresolved): App. 11 seven apparent survivors from 1988 pups against App. 7 five yearlings in 1989; App. 12 ten tagged survivors from the 1994 risk set of 15 against App. 7 seven total adults in 1995 (these entries cannot jointly be read as consistent counts of the same survivors at one census; the reason is unresolved); App. 11 seven tagged pups in 1996 against App. 7 two male plus two female; App. 7 59 pups against p. 47's 58; App. 16 has 43 numbered records, N = 42, fates 10 dead + 28 survived + 3 unknown, published 28/38, and 15 Haley rows cover 11 named animals. Transplant: pp. 50–51 report four overwinter deaths at Mount McQuillan; App. 16 names Franklin with one predation death and three hibernation or disease deaths; linkage unresolved and the donor colony unknown (unknown origin is not evidence that Haley was not the donor).
- Reproduction and movement context: reproduction is concentrated among a few females (App. 14, with survival selection). Nine arrivals are not net movement and should not be set against mean adult abundance as if sharing a denominator. These summaries do not isolate abundance feedback or any single mechanism (Haley review).
