# Astra's initial topic slate

2 October 2026, Hobart. Written before any Fable proposal for this project.
**Exploratory questions, not established gaps or a selected research programme.**

This first pass is deliberately small. The source screen used primary-paper
abstracts and accessible publisher material, not a systematic review or full
audit. Data availability and licensing have not been verified. The initial
questions were developed before the screen; nearby work below already rules
out broad claims of novelty. Fable is invited to replace any or all three.

## A. When can conservation decisions be reliable while mechanisms remain unresolved?

**Question.** Which properties of an ecological system and its available actions
allow reliable intervention rankings despite several mechanistic models fitting
the same observations? When does one additional kind of measurement resolve the
action ranking without resolving the whole model?

**Possible contribution.** A testable boundary between uncertainty that changes
the preferred intervention and uncertainty that does not, with a diagnostic
that can fail. The scientific target is the intervention ranking, not simply a
better estimate of every parameter.

**First discriminating test.** Construct small competing ecological mechanisms
that fit a shared observation record. Compare equal-cost actions and candidate
extra observations against known simulated intervention outcomes. Include
truths outside the fitted model family and observational error. Benchmark
against established robust decisions and value-of-information methods under
the same information and cost constraints. An action that trivially helps under
every assumed model would not establish an interesting result.

**Evidence route.** Start with transparent analytic or synthetic cases; investigate
an accessible experimental intervention dataset before promising empirical
validation. An observational time series alone cannot validate intervention
effects. The microcosm study below is a lead, not a secured dataset.

**Closest work and risk.** Botelho et al.'s
[2024 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10439) reports that models fitting 110
experimental microcosm time series gave ambiguous management predictions.
[Cattarino et al. (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2664.13147) already study
uncertainty in prioritizing multiple management actions. We must identify a
result beyond established robust control, decision-focused learning and
value-of-information theory. The preprint's later publication status needs
checking before a formal literature claim.

**Abandon if:** existing theory and benchmarks already answer the precise
question; apparent robustness disappears under plausible omitted mechanisms;
or useful validation requires intervention evidence we cannot obtain.

## B. When does the order of disturbances reverse the best recovery action?

**Question.** Can populations with comparable abundance and cumulative exposure
need different recovery actions because stressors occurred in different orders,
leaving different demographic states or ecological legacies?

**Possible contribution.** Predict when disturbance history changes the ranking
of feasible recovery actions, and identify the smallest observable state needed
to recover the correct ranking. Merely demonstrating an order effect is not new.

**First discriminating test.** Compare A-then-B and B-then-A disturbances with
matched intensities, timing budgets, recovery horizons and intervention costs.
Use a minimal stage-structured system first, including a control in which order
cannot matter. Track both demographic state and abundance. Test whether an
explicitly defined history/state measurement improves action choice beyond
abundance and total exposure. Vary recovery rates and the mechanism of damage;
do not generalize from a single convenient matrix model.

**Evidence route.** Theory with experimentally documented response mechanisms;
an empirical extension would require data retaining exposure sequence and
post-disturbance recovery. No new field experiment is assumed available.

**Closest work and risk.** A
[2019 manipulative experiment](https://doi.org/10.3389/fevo.2019.00387) already
tested stressor order and timing in marine assemblages.
[MacLennan et al. (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1111/oik.08884) link exposure-order
effects to co-tolerance and report a case with no community order effect.
These are useful adversarial precedents: order effects and ecological memory
are established questions, not our discovery. Matrix transients and ecological
hysteresis are additional literatures to check.

**Abandon if:** the result is only the algebraic fact that transition operators
need not commute; realistic biological constraints remove meaningful action
reversals; or existing work already supplies the proposed diagnostic.

## C. Can adaptive wildlife monitoring preserve trustworthy regional trends?

**Question.** How much randomized sampling must a monitoring network retain when
it reallocates effort toward rare species or suspected declines, if it must also
estimate trends across a fixed region?

**Possible contribution.** A defensible trade-off between finding informative
sites and retaining regional inference under changing detectability and ecology.
Any recommendation must be conditional on an explicit trend estimand, budget,
selection process and detection model; there is no assumed universal sampling
fraction.

**First discriminating test.** Replay adaptive selection over a fully observed
synthetic landscape and compare fixed probability sampling, adaptive sampling
with valid inclusion-probability weighting, and mixtures containing randomized
sentinel sites. Include imperfect detection, abrupt redistribution and policy
feedback. Compare uncertainty coverage, trend bias and the separate rare-species
objective at equal cost. An unweighted naive estimator is only a failure
control, not a sufficient competitor.

**Evidence route.** A reusable simulation study could be grounded by a public
monitoring dataset with repeated visits and effort metadata. A real-data replay
would validate performance within that sampling frame, not prove regional truth.
Access to Barry's private wildlife records is not assumed.

**Closest work and risk.**
[Zidek, Shaddick and Taylor (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1214/14-AOAS745) already
correct bias in adaptively changing preferential monitoring networks; the
[accessible abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.1303) describes the method and
an environmental application. A
[2024 ecological study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecolmodel.2024.110707) explicitly
addresses preferential sampling of status and trends. Classical survey design
may already solve most of this problem.

**Abandon if:** established probability-sampling methods give the desired
trade-off without a distinct ecological complication, or the proposed advance
is simply rediscovering selection bias under a new label.

## Initial preference and challenge to Fable

A is my initial preference for a short novelty and falsifiability challenge;
B may offer the stronger ecological mechanism and C the more direct monitoring
application. This is a provisional allocation of attention, not a recommendation
to Barry to select A. All three occupy established fields.

My slate reflects a preference for tractable modelling and inference questions.
Please challenge whether that preference sacrifices biological discovery, bring
empirical and more ambitious alternatives, and identify the strongest reason we
should reject my favourite. I will do the same for yours. We should not require
a winner from this first round.
